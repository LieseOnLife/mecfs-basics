>This page explains what research found and what people report. It doesn't recommend treatments. Talk through any change with a clinician who knows ME/CFS.

> [!summary] Quick answer
> Myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS) is a serious, chronic, systemic disease that often dramatically limits what people can do.[[Beyond Myalgic Encephalomyelitis-Chronic Fatigue Syndrome Redefining an Illness#^q1|IOM 2015]] It affects multiple body systems, and its biology is still being investigated.[[Myalgic encephalomyelitis (or encephalopathy)-chronic fatigue syndrome diagnosis and management NG206#^q6|NICE 2021]] The core symptoms are reduced ability to function with fatigue, post-exertional malaise (PEM), unrefreshing sleep, and cognitive problems and/or orthostatic intolerance.[[Caring for Patients with ME-CFS Clinical Care and Treatment for Health Care Providers#^q5|CDC 2024]][[Caring for Patients with ME-CFS Clinical Care and Treatment for Health Care Providers#^q6|q6]][[Caring for Patients with ME-CFS Clinical Care and Treatment for Health Care Providers#^q7|q7]][[Caring for Patients with ME-CFS Clinical Care and Treatment for Health Care Providers#^q8|q8]] Symptoms fluctuate,[[Myalgic encephalomyelitis (or encephalopathy)-chronic fatigue syndrome diagnosis and management NG206#^q7|NICE 2021]] and severity ranges from mild to very severe, where people are in bed all day and depend on care.[[Myalgic encephalomyelitis (or encephalopathy)-chronic fatigue syndrome diagnosis and management NG206#^q1|NICE 2021]][[Myalgic encephalomyelitis (or encephalopathy)-chronic fatigue syndrome diagnosis and management NG206#^q2|q2]][[Myalgic encephalomyelitis (or encephalopathy)-chronic fatigue syndrome diagnosis and management NG206#^q3|q3]][[Myalgic encephalomyelitis (or encephalopathy)-chronic fatigue syndrome diagnosis and management NG206#^q4|q4]][[Myalgic encephalomyelitis (or encephalopathy)-chronic fatigue syndrome diagnosis and management NG206#^q5|q5]]

## The core symptoms
The US diagnostic criteria from the 2015 Institute of Medicine (IOM) report require three symptoms plus at least one of two more.[^iom-structure] The CDC's clinical pages describe the same structure.[^cdc-criteria]

| Symptom                                      | What it means                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Reduced function with fatigue                | A substantial drop from pre-illness levels of work, school, social, or personal activity lasting more than 6 months, with fatigue that is new, not caused by ongoing overexertion, and not substantially relieved by rest.[^iom-criteria]     |
| Post-exertional malaise (PEM)                | Symptoms and function get worse after physical or mental exertion that used to be tolerated.[^iom-pem] It's the most distinctive symptom; see [[Post-exertional malaise\|Post-exertional malaise]].[^coal-hallmark]                                         |
| Unrefreshing sleep                           | The IOM calls the complaint universal among patients when asked about specifically.[^iom-sleep] NICE's description: waking exhausted, flu-like and stiff; broken or shallow sleep, altered sleep patterns, or sleeping too much.[^nice-sleep] |
| Cognitive impairment *(one of these two)*    | Problems with thinking and executive function that worsen with exertion, effort, stress, or time pressure; slowed information processing is common.[^iom-cognitive] Patients often call it "brain fog".[^coal-brainfog]                       |
| Orthostatic intolerance *(one of these two)* | Symptoms worsen when upright and improve, though not always fully, when lying back down or raising the feet.[^iom-oi]                                                                                                                         |

The Clinician Coalition says these core features must be moderate to severe and present at least 50% of the time, and that this is key to separating ME/CFS from other common causes of chronic fatigue.[^coal-hallmark] The IOM applies a similar threshold (at least half the time, at moderate, substantial, or severe intensity) to PEM, unrefreshing sleep, and cognitive impairment.[^iom-half]

## How severe it gets
NICE, the UK's guideline body, describes four levels, while noting that the lines aren't clear-cut because individual symptoms vary.[^nice-severity]

| Level       | What daily life looks like (NICE)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mild        | Cares for themselves and does light domestic tasks; most still work or study, often by dropping leisure and social pursuits, working reduced hours, and using the weekend to cope with the rest of the week.[^nice-mild]                                                                                                      |
| Moderate    | Reduced mobility and restricted in all daily activities; has usually stopped work or education and often rests 1 to 2 hours in the afternoon.[^nice-moderate]                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Severe      | Unable to do any activity for themselves, or can do minimal daily tasks only (such as washing their face); severe cognitive difficulties; often can't leave the house, or have a severe and prolonged after-effect if they do; may spend most of the time in bed; often extremely sensitive to light and sound.[^nice-severe] |
| Very severe | In bed all day and dependent on care, including for hygiene and eating; very sensitive to sensory stimuli; some can't swallow and need tube feeding.[^nice-very-severe]                                                                                                                                                       |

Symptoms can change unpredictably over a day, a week, or longer.[^nice-fluctuate] The CDC estimates that up to 1 in 4 people with ME/CFS are home- or bed-bound at some point,[^cdc-housebound] and the US ME/CFS Clinician Coalition reports that up to 75% can't work or attend school and that most patients never regain their pre-illness functioning.[^coal-burden]

## How it starts and who gets it
Onset is often sudden, and patients frequently report that an infection-like illness, such as mononucleosis or flu-like illness, came first.[^coal-onset] The cause is unknown.[^coal-cause] The IOM report notes that symptoms may be triggered by certain infections, such as Epstein-Barr virus.[^iom-cause]

- **How many:** an estimated 836,000 to 2.5 million Americans, of whom 84 to 91 percent haven't been diagnosed, so the true number is unknown.[^iom-prevalence]
- **Who:** about 3 times as many women as men; one peak of onset between ages 11 and 19 and another between 30 and 39.[^coal-who] The average age of onset is 33, though cases have been reported under 10 and over 70.[^iom-age]
- **Doctors' knowledge:** fewer than a third of medical school curricula and less than half of medical textbooks include ME/CFS.[^iom-training]

## What it is, and isn't
The Clinician Coalition describes how ME/CFS used to be characterized as medically unexplained fatigue that responds to talk therapy and graded exercise, and says scientific advances now show a complex multisystem disease involving neurological, immune, autonomic, and energy-metabolism problems.[^coal-multisystem] It tells clinicians to explain to patients and families that ME/CFS is a serious medical illness, not laziness, depression, or a psychosomatic disorder.[^coal-validate] The history of that shift is on [[Reading the research|Reading the research]].

There are therapies for managing symptoms, but how well they work isn't well understood, and there is no cure.[^iom-cure] The IOM proposed a new name, systemic exertion intolerance disease (SEID), to capture how exertion of any kind can affect multiple organ systems.[^iom-seid] That name didn't replace ME/CFS: the CDC's 2024 clinical pages, for example, still use ME/CFS.[^cdc-name]

ME/CFS also affects families and carers: daily activities, family and social life, emotional wellbeing, work, and education.[^nice-families] See [[Daily life and caring at home|Daily life and caring at home]].
