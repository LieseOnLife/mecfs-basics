Terms in alphabetical order, with a link to the page that explains each one.

### 2-day CPET
Two cardiopulmonary exercise tests 24 hours apart: the first measures baseline capacity and acts as a stressor; the second measures how function changed after it.[^stevens] Explained in [[Post-exertional malaise#The evidence: two-day exercise testing|The evidence: two-day exercise testing]].

### Brain fog
Patients' name for cognitive impairment in ME/CFS.[^coal-fog] Explained in [[What ME-CFS is#The core symptoms|The core symptoms]].

### Canadian Consensus Criteria (CCC)
A clinical case definition requiring fatigue, post-exertional malaise and/or fatigue, sleep dysfunction, and pain; two or more neurological/cognitive symptoms; symptoms from two of the autonomic, neuroendocrine, and immune categories; and illness lasting at least six months (three months for children).[^ccc] See [[Reading the research#Case definitions: who counts as having ME/CFS|Case definitions: who counts as having ME/CFS]].

### Care and support plan
NICE's term for a personalised plan agreed with the person (and family or carers, if appropriate), covering daily living support, aids, energy management, symptom and medicines management, and contacts.[^nice-plan] Explained in [[Diagnosis and getting care#What guidelines ask of clinicians|What guidelines ask of clinicians]].

### Carer's assessment
In UK carer guidance, an assessment of the impact caring has on an unpaid carer and how they can be supported.[^carers-assessment] See [[Daily life and caring at home#Looking after yourself as a carer|Looking after yourself as a carer]].

### Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT)
A therapy that NICE says should only be offered to help people manage symptoms, improve functioning, and reduce distress, not as a cure.[^nice-cbt] See [[Reading the research#The PACE trial and the graded exercise story|The PACE trial and the graded exercise story]].

### Crash
What patients often call PEM: a worsening of symptoms and function after exertion, often delayed by hours or days.[^coal-crash] Explained in [[Post-exertional malaise#What PEM is|What PEM is]].

### Energy envelope
The individual limits for mental and physical activity that people plan activity and rest within.[^cdc-envelope] Explained in [[Daily life and caring at home#Pacing and the energy envelope|Pacing and the energy envelope]].

### Energy management
NICE's term for a person-led, non-curative strategy for using available energy while reducing the risk of PEM.[^nice-em] See [[Daily life and caring at home#Pacing and the energy envelope|Pacing and the energy envelope]].

### Fukuda criteria
The 1994 Fukuda case definition, under which PEM is optional.[^vink-fukuda] See [[Reading the research#Case definitions: who counts as having ME/CFS|Case definitions: who counts as having ME/CFS]].

### Graded exercise therapy (GET)
As NICE defines it: establishing a baseline of achievable activity, then making fixed incremental increases in time spent physically active.[^nice-get] NICE says not to offer it.[^nice-noget] See [[Reading the research#The PACE trial and the graded exercise story|The PACE trial and the graded exercise story]].

### IOM criteria
The Institute of Medicine diagnostic criteria: reduced function with fatigue lasting more than 6 months, PEM, and unrefreshing sleep, plus cognitive impairment or orthostatic intolerance, with symptoms present at least half the time at moderate or greater intensity.[^iom-criteria] Explained in [[What ME-CFS is#The core symptoms|The core symptoms]].

### International Consensus Criteria (ICC)
A case definition in which postexertional neuroimmune exhaustion (PENE) is compulsory, plus symptoms across neurological, immune, and energy-metabolism categories.[^icc-def] See [[Reading the research#Case definitions: who counts as having ME/CFS|Case definitions: who counts as having ME/CFS]].

### NASA Lean Test
A standing test used to assess orthostatic intolerance.[^coal-lean] See [[Diagnosis and getting care#How diagnosis works|How diagnosis works]].

### Orthostatic intolerance
Symptoms that worsen when upright and improve (not always fully) when lying down or raising the feet.[^iom-oi] Explained in [[What ME-CFS is#The core symptoms|The core symptoms]].

### Oxford criteria
A definition requiring fatigue as the main symptom, with significant disability and no exclusionary medical or psychiatric diagnosis; used in the PACE trial.[^pace-oxford] See [[Reading the research#Case definitions: who counts as having ME/CFS|Case definitions: who counts as having ME/CFS]].

### Pacing
An individualised way of managing physical, cognitive, and emotional energy within a person's limits.[^coal-pacing] Explained in [[Daily life and caring at home#Pacing and the energy envelope|Pacing and the energy envelope]].

### Post-exertional malaise (PEM)
Worsening of symptoms and function after physical or cognitive stress that was tolerated before the illness.[^iom-pem] Explained in [[Post-exertional malaise#What PEM is|What PEM is]].

### Postexertional neuroimmune exhaustion (PENE)
The ICC's compulsory feature: a pathological inability to produce enough energy on demand, with marked, rapid fatigability after exertion.[^icc] See [[Reading the research#Case definitions: who counts as having ME/CFS|Case definitions: who counts as having ME/CFS]].

### POTS (postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome)
Also called postural tachycardia syndrome. A form of orthostatic intolerance listed among autonomic symptoms in the CCC;[^ccc-pots] clinicians can check for it with a standing or tilt test.[^iom-pots] See [[Diagnosis and getting care#How diagnosis works|How diagnosis works]].

### Push-and-crash cycle
Doing too much on a "good day" to make up for lost time, which can lead to relapse.[^cdc-pushcrash] See [[Daily life and caring at home#Pacing and the energy envelope|Pacing and the energy envelope]].

### SEID
"Systemic exertion intolerance disease", the name the IOM committee proposed to replace ME/CFS.[^iom-seid] See [[What ME-CFS is#What it is, and isn't|What it is, and isn't]].

### Severity levels
NICE's four levels (mild, moderate, severe, very severe), based on the impact on everyday functioning.[^nice-severity] Explained in [[What ME-CFS is#How severe it gets|How severe it gets]].

### Unrefreshing sleep
NICE groups this as "unrefreshing sleep or sleep disturbance": waking exhausted, flu-like, and stiff, or broken or shallow sleep, an altered sleep pattern, or hypersomnia.[^nice-sleep] Explained in [[What ME-CFS is#The core symptoms|The core symptoms]].

### Ventilatory/anaerobic threshold (VAT)
A threshold in an exercise test; one study describes oxygen uptake at this point (VO2@VAT) as representing a pivotal shift in oxygen transport, and possibly energy metabolism.[^keller-vat] See [[Post-exertional malaise#The evidence: two-day exercise testing|The evidence: two-day exercise testing]].
