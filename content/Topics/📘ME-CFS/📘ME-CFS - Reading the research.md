# Reading the research

>[!warning] Evidence log, not medical advice
>This page explains what research found and what people report. It doesn't recommend treatments. Talk through any change with a clinician who knows ME/CFS.

> [!summary] Quick answer
> Studies define ME/CFS differently. Newer definitions require post-exertional malaise (ICC, IOM) or "post-exertional malaise and/or fatigue" (CCC),[[🔬Beyond Myalgic Encephalomyelitis-Chronic Fatigue Syndrome Redefining an Illness -by- Institute of Medicine#^q12|IOM 2015]][[🔬Beyond Myalgic Encephalomyelitis-Chronic Fatigue Syndrome Redefining an Illness -by- Institute of Medicine#^q13|q13]][[🔬Beyond Myalgic Encephalomyelitis-Chronic Fatigue Syndrome Redefining an Illness -by- Institute of Medicine#^q14|q14]][[🔬Beyond Myalgic Encephalomyelitis-Chronic Fatigue Syndrome Redefining an Illness -by- Institute of Medicine#^q15|q15]][[🔬Beyond Myalgic Encephalomyelitis-Chronic Fatigue Syndrome Redefining an Illness -by- Institute of Medicine#^q32|q32]] [[🔬Myalgic encephalomyelitis International Consensus Criteria -by- Bruce M. Carruthers#^q3|Carruthers 2011]] [[🔬Myalgic Encephalomyelitis-Chronic Fatigue Syndrome Clinical Working Case Definition, Diagnostic and Treatment Protocols -by- Bruce M. Carruthers#^q2|Carruthers 2003]][[🔬Myalgic Encephalomyelitis-Chronic Fatigue Syndrome Clinical Working Case Definition, Diagnostic and Treatment Protocols -by- Bruce M. Carruthers#^q3|q3]] while the 1994 Fukuda definition treats PEM as optional[[🔬Graded exercise therapy for ME-CFS is not effective and unsafe - re-analysis of a Cochrane review -by- Mark Vink#^q2|Vink 2018]] and the Oxford definition centres on fatigue.[[🔬PACE trial of adaptive pacing, CBT, graded exercise and specialist medical care for CFS -by- Peter D White#^q2|White 2011 (PACE)]] The large PACE trial (Oxford criteria) reported that CBT and graded exercise therapy (GET) moderately improved outcomes,[[🔬PACE trial of adaptive pacing, CBT, graded exercise and specialist medical care for CFS -by- Peter D White#^q6|White 2011 (PACE)]] but a reanalysis using the trial's original protocol found the advantage over control wasn't significant after correcting for multiple comparisons.[[🔬Rethinking the treatment of CFS - a reanalysis of the PACE trial -by- Carolyn E Wilshire#^q2|Wilshire 2018]] NICE's 2021 guideline says not to offer GET,[[🔬Myalgic encephalomyelitis (or encephalopathy)-chronic fatigue syndrome diagnosis and management NG206 -by- National Institute for Health and Care Excellence#^q122|NICE 2021]][[🔬Myalgic encephalomyelitis (or encephalopathy)-chronic fatigue syndrome diagnosis and management NG206 -by- National Institute for Health and Care Excellence#^q86|q86]][[🔬Myalgic encephalomyelitis (or encephalopathy)-chronic fatigue syndrome diagnosis and management NG206 -by- National Institute for Health and Care Excellence#^q87|q87]] and to offer CBT only as support for managing symptoms, not as a cure.[[🔬Myalgic encephalomyelitis (or encephalopathy)-chronic fatigue syndrome diagnosis and management NG206 -by- National Institute for Health and Care Excellence#^q90|NICE 2021]]

## Case definitions: who counts as having ME/CFS
| Definition | What it requires (from the source) | PEM required? |
|---|---|---|
| Oxford | Fatigue as the main symptom, with significant disability, and no exclusionary medical or psychiatric diagnosis (as quoted in the PACE trial).[^pace-oxford] | Not among its requirements |
| Fukuda / CDC (1994) | PEM is one of eight additional criteria, so it's optional, not compulsory.[^vink-fukuda] | No |
| Canadian Consensus Criteria, CCC (2003) | Fatigue, "post-exertional malaise and/or fatigue", sleep dysfunction, and pain, plus neurological/cognitive and other symptoms, for at least six months (three months for children).[^ccc-def] [^ccc-duration] | Yes (as PEM and/or fatigue) |
| International Consensus Criteria, ICC (2011) | "Postexertional neuroimmune exhaustion" (PENE) is compulsory, plus symptoms across neurological, immune, and energy-metabolism categories.[^icc-core] [^icc-pene] | Yes |
| IOM (2015) | Reduced function with fatigue for more than 6 months, PEM, and unrefreshing sleep, plus cognitive impairment or orthostatic intolerance.[^iom-pem-required] | Yes |

**Why it matters:** a study can only tell you about the people it enrolled. A review by Vink, critical of exercise trials, notes that PEM is optional under Fukuda and cites a study finding that 15 per cent of people labelled as having CFS by those criteria were healthy.[^vink-fukuda] The Cochrane review of exercise therapy says all its included studies used the 1994 CDC (Fukuda) or Oxford criteria, or both, and that patients diagnosed using other criteria may experience different effects.[^larun-criteria] That's why these docs give the case definition and whether PEM was required in footnotes for cohort studies.

## What to look for in a study
- **The case definition**, as above.
- **Blinding and who rates the outcome.** In PACE, participants, therapists, and doctors couldn't be blinded to treatment, and the primary outcomes were rated by participants themselves.[^pace-blinding] The Cochrane review rated all its exercise trials at high risk of performance and detection bias.[^larun-bias] Vink argues that unblinded trials with self-reported outcomes are prone to pronounced bias.[^vink-blinding]
- **Harms and dropouts.** Vink says harms weren't reported adequately in the exercise trials,[^vink-abstract] and gives examples where more people dropped out of exercise groups than control groups.[^vink-dropouts]
- **Sample size.** Small studies are less certain; the largest 2-day exercise-test study names its sample size as its main limitation,[^keller-limits] and an NIH deep-phenotyping study notes its small sample may affect precision, with a propensity to inflate estimates.[^walitt-limits]
- **Study type.** A survey of people's experiences is different from a controlled trial: one large patient survey reported more people worse after GET than better, but its authors say much more detailed research is needed.[^geraghty-results] [^geraghty-caveat]
- **Conflicts of interest.** Authors of the largest 2-day CPET study run that test for a fee.[^keller-coi]

## The PACE trial and the graded exercise story
**PACE (2011)** randomised 641 people from six UK specialist clinics, selected by the Oxford criteria, to specialist medical care (SMC) alone or with adaptive pacing therapy, CBT, or GET.[^pace-design] [^pace-oxford] At 52 weeks, fatigue scores were 3.4 points lower with CBT and 3.2 points lower with GET than with specialist medical care alone, with no difference for adaptive pacing.[^pace-findings] The authors concluded that CBT and GET "can safely be added to SMC to moderately improve outcomes", and that adaptive pacing was not an effective addition.[^pace-interpretation]

**The reanalysis (Wilshire 2018)** used trial data released under a Freedom of Information request to follow the original protocol's outcomes.[^wilshire-methods] By the protocol's definition, 20% of CBT patients, 21% of GET patients, and 10% of controls improved, but CBT and GET didn't significantly beat control after correcting for the number of planned comparisons.[^wilshire-results] [^wilshire-improvement] Recovery rates were 7%, 4%, and 3%, with no significant differences,[^wilshire-recovery] and in the long-term follow-up data they analysed, the small group differences were no longer evident.[^wilshire-longterm] The authors conclude the modest effects on self-report measures don't exceed what reporting bias could explain.[^wilshire-conclusion]

**The Cochrane review (Larun et al.)** pooled 8 trials with 1,518 participants and concluded that exercise therapy probably reduces fatigue at the end of treatment compared with usual care or passive therapies (moderate-certainty evidence), with uncertain long-term effects and uncertain evidence on adverse effects.[^larun-findings] [^larun-conclusion] Vink's re-analysis of the 2017 version argues there is no evidence that GET is effective and that, because harms weren't adequately reported, it can't be called safe.[^vink-abstract]

**What changed:** NICE's 2021 guideline defines GET as fixed incremental increases in physical activity from a baseline,[^nice-getdef] and says not to offer it, or any programme based on deconditioning and exercise-avoidance theories.[^nice-noget] It says CBT has sometimes been assumed to be a cure, but should only support people in managing symptoms and the distress of a chronic illness,[^nice-cbt] and should be offered only if the person wants it after discussion.[^nice-cbt-offer] The CDC says standard exercise recommendations for healthy people can substantially harm people with ME/CFS.[^cdc-exercise]

## Why prevalence estimates differ
- The IOM's 2015 estimate was 836,000 to 2.5 million Americans, with 84 to 91 percent undiagnosed.[^iom-prevalence]
- A CDC survey of 57,133 US adults in 2021–2022 found 1.3% had ME/CFS,[^nchs-prevalence] [^nchs-methods] counting people who said a health professional had diagnosed it and they still had it.[^nchs-ascertainment] Because undiagnosed people aren't counted, the report says true prevalence may be higher.[^nchs-limit]

The IOM figure includes an estimate of undiagnosed people;[^iom-prevalence] the survey counts only self-reported diagnoses,[^nchs-ascertainment] so the two aren't directly comparable.
