# Summary
This is the largest two-day cardiopulmonary exercise test (2-day CPET) study of ME/CFS. 84 people meeting the Canadian Consensus Criteria (CCC) and 71 sedentary controls each did two maximal cycling tests 24 hours apart. Controls reproduced their day-1 results on day 2. The ME/CFS group did not: work, oxygen uptake, ventilation, heart rate, and related measures fell on day 2, at peak effort and at the ventilatory/anaerobic threshold (VAT). The pattern held in 55 pairs matched for sex, age, and baseline aerobic capacity, so the authors argue that fitness level does not explain it. They then translate the day-2 results into a more severe impairment classification and discuss non-drug management ideas, which they did not test.

# Methods
Data were collected from 2018 to 2022 at Ithaca College (Ithaca, NY), Weill Cornell Medicine (New York, NY), and ID Med (Torrance, CA). Recruitment ran through ME/CFS clinics, including Susan Levine's practice in NYC, plus advocacy sites and ClinicalTrials.gov. Screening included a physical exam, labs, and confirmation that patients met CCC, which requires PEM. Both CPETs used a cycle ergometer, 24 hours apart. Measures at rest, VAT, and peak were compared by two-way repeated-measures ANOVA, and test-to-test stability by intraclass correlation. Matched pairs were matched first by sex, then age, then day-1 peak VO2. About 75% of both groups were female, and most patients were mild to moderate on the Bell Activity Scale. The trial was registered retrospectively as `NCT04026425`. Funding was mainly the NIH U54 ME/CFS Collaborative Research Center grant (U54NS105541).

# Findings
- **Peak effort:** On day 2 the ME/CFS group showed significant declines in work, exercise time, ventilation, VO2, VCO2, tidal volume, heart rate, O2 pulse, diastolic blood pressure, and rate-pressure product. Controls showed no such declines.
- **At VAT:** Ventilatory efficiency (VE/VCO2), end-tidal CO2, O2 pulse, work, VO2, and systolic pressure declined on day 2 in ME/CFS. In the matched pairs, VO2 at VAT fell 6.7% from day 1 to day 2 in ME/CFS, with no change in controls.
- **Matched pairs:** Peak VO2 fell 6% on day 2 in ME/CFS with no change in controls, even though the pairs were matched on day-1 peak VO2.
- **Effort:** Rated perceived exertion met maximal-effort criteria on both days in both groups, which argues against low effort explaining the day-2 drop.
- **Stability and impairment:** CPET values were more stable across days in controls than in ME/CFS. Classified by day-2 data, the ME/CFS group's impairment status was more severe than by day-1 data.
- **Management ideas:** The discussion proposes untested, non-drug approaches. Most concrete is pacing with a heart-rate-monitor alarm set about 10 bpm below the heart rate at VAT, or, when that isn't known, resting heart rate plus 15 bpm or a low perceived-exertion ceiling. The section also mentions compression garments, transcutaneous vagus nerve stimulation, whole-body cryotherapy, photobiomodulation, slow breathing, and blood-flow-restriction training as ideas to study.

# Limitations / flags
- **Conflict of interest:** Several authors (BK, CR, JS, SS) run 2-day CPETs on a fee-for-service basis, hence `conflict-of-interest`.
- **Sample and effect sizes:** The authors name sample size as the main limitation and say many effect sizes were small to moderate. They note that a larger single-day CPET study (MCAM, Cook et al.) found fewer differences.
- **Generalizability:** Most patients were mild to moderate, and the ME/CFS group had a higher share of Caucasian participants than controls. The authors say results may not apply to severely ill people or to all races and ethnicities. Sex differences were not analyzed, and registration was retrospective.
- **Treatments:** The management section draws on clinical experience and indirect evidence, not outcomes from this study. Only pacing is linked, because the heart-rate-threshold advice comes directly from this study's VAT data; the other approaches are listed above but not linked.

# Abstract
**Background:** Post-exertional malaise (PEM), the hallmark symptom of myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS), represents a constellation of abnormal responses to physical, cognitive, and/or emotional exertion including profound fatigue, cognitive dysfunction, and exertion intolerance, among numerous other maladies. Two sequential cardiopulmonary exercise tests (2-d CPET) provide objective evidence of abnormal responses to exertion in ME/CFS but validated only in studies with small sample sizes. Further, translation of results to impairment status and approaches to symptom reduction are lacking.

**Methods:** Participants with ME/CFS (Canadian Criteria; n = 84) and sedentary controls (CTL; n = 71) completed two CPETs on a cycle ergometer separated by 24 h. Two-way repeated measures ANOVA compared CPET measures at rest, ventilatory/anaerobic threshold (VAT), and peak effort between phenotypes and CPETs. Intraclass correlations described stability of CPET measures across tests, and relevant objective CPET data indicated impairment status. A subset of case–control pairs (n = 55) matched for aerobic capacity, age, and sex, were also analyzed. ^q1

**Results:** Unlike CTL, ME/CFS failed to reproduce CPET-1 measures during CPET-2 with significant declines at peak exertion in work, exercise time, VE, VO2, VCO2, VT, HR, O2pulse, DBP, and RPP. Likewise, CPET-2 declines were observed at VAT for VE/VCO2, PetCO2, O2pulse, work, VO2 and SBP. Perception of effort (RPE) exceeded maximum effort criteria for ME/CFS and CTL on both CPETs. Results were similar in matched pairs. Intraclass correlations revealed greater stability in CPET variables across test days in CTL compared to ME/CFS owing to CPET-2 declines in ME/CFS. Lastly, CPET-2 data signaled more severe impairment status for ME/CFS compared to CPET-1. ^q2

**Conclusions:** Presently, this is the largest 2-d CPET study of ME/CFS to substantiate impaired recovery in ME/CFS following an exertional stressor. Abnormal post-exertional CPET responses persisted compared to CTL matched for aerobic capacity, indicating that fitness level does not predispose to exertion intolerance in ME/CFS. Moreover, contributions to exertion intolerance in ME/CFS by disrupted cardiac, pulmonary, and metabolic factors implicates autonomic nervous system dysregulation of blood flow and oxygen delivery for energy metabolism. The observable declines in post-exertional energy metabolism translate notably to a worsening of impairment status. Treatment considerations to address tangible reductions in physiological function are proffered. ^q3

**Trial registration:** ClinicalTrials.gov, retrospectively registered, ID# NCT04026425, date of registration: 2019-07-17.

## Methods
The CPET protocol was an incremental, ramping workload on an accommodating-resistance electronically-braked cycle ergometer. Following 3 min of seated rest on the ergometer, the exercise protocol began at 0 Watts (W) with an incremental rise in work intensity of 15 W throughout each minute of exercise at the rate of 5W over 20 s. Participants completed two maximum effort CPETs separated by 24 h. ^q4

## Results
In the matched-pairs sample, a comparison of CPET-1 to CPET-2 for ME/CFS revealed persistent declines in 8 of 11 CPET measures in the total ME/CFS sample discussed above, with V˙T, O2pulse, and DBP no longer significantly different between tests. However, two cardiovascular measures (HR, RPP) that decreased on CPET-2 (*p* ≤ 0.05) in the total sample were also lower on CPET-2 (*p* ≤ 0.01) in ME/CFS in the matched-pairs. Thus, the post-exertional deterioration of CPET measures during CPET-2 further corroborates the impact of PEM on oxygen delivery and energy production in ME/CFS. In contrast, only one measure (V˙CO2) for CTL in the matched-pairs decreased from CPET-1 to CPET-2 (*p* ≤ 0.01), further supporting the well-established high reliability and consistency of peak CPET measures [[51](#CR51)], and consistent with the comparatively high intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC) for CTL discussed below (Table [5](#Tab5)). ^q5

Although pairs were matched for peak V˙O2, V˙O2@VAT was 15% higher in CTL for CPET-1 (*p* ≤ 0.01) and 20% higher for CPET-2 (*p* ≤ 0.01). This was due to a 6.7% decline in V˙O2@VAT in ME/CFS from CPET-1 to CPET-2 (*p* ≤ 0.05), with no change for CTL. These results indicate that V˙O2@VAT represents a pivotal shift in O2 transport, and possibly energy metabolism, that may confer a greater negative impact on peripheral oxygen extraction (O2pulse) with a higher perception of effort (RPE) in ME/CFS compared to CTL. ^q6

## Limitations
Although the largest study to date of ME/CFS completing a 2-d CPET protocol, the primary limitation of this study is, in fact, the sample size. The disparate findings of this study compared to the larger 1-d CPET study of ME/CFS [[30](#CR30)] suggests that a larger sample size is needed to more fully characterize the post-exertional impact of exercise on ME/CFS. Results of the present study notwithstanding, the effect sizes for many highly significant differences between phenotypes and between CPETs were small to moderate, possibly qualifying the practical application of the findings. However, given that post-exertional declines in oxygen consumption, ventilation, hemodynamic measures, and work, particularly at ventilatory/anaerobic threshold, are consistent with previous reports of 2-d CPET [[5](#CR5), [19](#CR19), [22](#CR22)–[26](#CR26), [28](#CR28), [92](#CR92), [96](#CR96), [112](#CR112), [113](#CR113)], the stability of the present results only begin to describe the true magnitude of post-exertional effects for those with ME/CFS. ^q7

This multi-site study afforded participant selection from urban, suburban, and rural locales. While participants included representation from multiple races and ethnicities, participants with ME/CFS were 90% + Caucasian. The proportion of females in this study with ME/CFS was similar to that reported elsewhere [[3](#CR3)], although sex differences were not evaluated in the analysis of the present data [[123](#CR123)]. In addition, ME/CFS participants were primarily of mild to moderate illness severity, indicated by the Bell Activity Scale [[35](#CR35)]. As such, the results of this study may not be generalizable to all races [[124](#CR124)], ethnicities, or those severely ill with ME/CFS. ^q8

## Conclusions
Baseline (CPET-1) aerobic capacity was similar and low in these groups of ME/CFS and inactive controls, although it was only ME/CFS that experienced a notable reduction in peak V˙O2 on CPET-2. However, it is the relevance of a lower baseline gas exchange threshold (VAT) that underscores the challenge of those with ME/CFS to accomplish daily activities without exceeding the VAT level of energy production and exacerbating illness symptoms. In addition to an array of illness symptoms that comprise the post-exertional response in ME/CFS, the reduction in VAT effectively shrinks the ‘energy envelope’ further reducing tolerance of energy-demanding activities. ^q9
