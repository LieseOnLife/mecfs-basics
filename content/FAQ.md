## Basics
**What is ME/CFS?**
A serious, chronic, systemic disease that often dramatically limits activity.[^iom-serious] → [[What ME-CFS is|What ME/CFS is]]

**What are the main symptoms?**
Under the IOM criteria: reduced function with fatigue for more than 6 months, PEM, and unrefreshing sleep, plus cognitive impairment or orthostatic intolerance.[^iom-criteria] → [[What ME-CFS is#The core symptoms|The core symptoms]]

**How severe can it get?**
From mild to very severe, where people are in bed all day and depend on care.[^nice-severity] → [[What ME-CFS is#How severe it gets|How severe it gets]]

**Is it a psychological illness?**
The Clinician Coalition tells clinicians to explain it's a serious medical illness, not laziness, depression, or a psychosomatic disorder.[^coal-validate] → [[What ME-CFS is#What it is, and isn't|What it is, and isn't]]

**What is post-exertional malaise (PEM)?**
A worsening of symptoms and function after exertion that used to be tolerated.[^iom-pem] → [[Post-exertional malaise|Post-exertional malaise]]

**How long after activity does a crash start?**
Often hours or days later;[^nice-pem] the CDC says typically 12 to 48 hours.[^cdc-pem] → [[Post-exertional malaise#When it starts (the delay)|When it starts (the delay)]]

## Care
**Is there a test for ME/CFS?**
No; it's diagnosed from history, examination, and tests that rule out other illnesses.[^cdc-notest] → [[Diagnosis and getting care#How diagnosis works|How diagnosis works]]

**How long do symptoms have to last before diagnosis?**
6 months or longer under the CDC and IOM criteria;[^cdc-sixmonths] [^iom-duration] 3 months under the UK's NICE guideline.[^nice-3months] → [[Diagnosis and getting care#How long before a diagnosis?|How long before a diagnosis?]]

**How can I help someone prepare for appointments?**
Because PEM may be delayed, the IOM suggests a diary of activities and symptoms for a week or two;[^iom-diary] the Coalition says a diary may help identify PEM.[^coal-diary] → [[Diagnosis and getting care#Helping someone prepare|Helping someone prepare]]

## Daily life
**What is pacing?**
Balancing rest and activity to stay within personal limits and avoid PEM.[^cdc-pacing] → [[Daily life and caring at home#Pacing and the energy envelope|Pacing and the energy envelope]]

**What helps during a crash?**
The Bateman Horne Center's guide makes encouraging rest and conserving energy the main goal, and also advises reducing stimuli and not expecting the person to talk.[^bhc-goal] → [[Daily life and caring at home#During a crash|During a crash]]

**What does caring for someone with severe ME/CFS involve?**
Pacing becomes the care team's job, with low-stimulus rest after care tasks and a detailed care plan.[^herm-team] → [[Daily life and caring at home#Caring for someone with severe or very severe ME/CFS|Caring for someone with severe or very severe ME/CFS]]

**How do carers look after themselves?**
The ME Association urges keeping a support network and taking breaks; the page covers more options.[^mea-network] → [[Daily life and caring at home#Looking after yourself as a carer|Looking after yourself as a carer]]

## Research
**Why do ME/CFS studies disagree?**
One reason: studies use different case definitions. The IOM criteria require PEM,[^iom-pem-req] while it's optional under Fukuda,[^vink-fukuda] which, with Oxford, was used in the exercise trials in the Cochrane review.[^larun-criteria] → [[Reading the research#Case definitions: who counts as having ME/CFS|Case definitions: who counts as having ME/CFS]]

**Why is graded exercise therapy no longer recommended?**
The exercise trials in the Cochrane review used Fukuda or Oxford criteria;[^larun-criteria] in the PACE trial, participants rated the primary outcomes themselves without blinding;[^pace-blinding] a reanalysis of PACE found CBT and GET didn't significantly beat control after correction.[^wilshire-results] NICE's 2021 guideline says not to offer GET.[^nice-noget] → [[Reading the research#The PACE trial and the graded exercise story|The PACE trial and the graded exercise story]]

**How many people have ME/CFS?**
The IOM estimated 836,000 to 2.5 million Americans, most undiagnosed;[^iom-prevalence] a 2021–2022 US survey found 1.3% of adults reported a diagnosis, which leaves out undiagnosed people.[^nchs] → [[Reading the research#Why prevalence estimates differ|Why prevalence estimates differ]]
