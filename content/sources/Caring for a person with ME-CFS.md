> [!info] About this source
> **What:** The ME Association's practical booklet, “Caring for a person with ME/CFS,” for people caring for an adult with ME/CFS; the PDF is dated July 2024.
> **Who:** The ME Association says the booklet provides a basic review of caring for an adult and includes real-life examples based on community feedback.
> **Strength:** It brings together practical descriptions of care needs, activity management, and support for carers.
> **Limits:** It is a charity leaflet, not a formal clinical guideline or systematic evidence review; its examples are community feedback, it focuses on adult care, and the booklet says it is not a substitute for personalized medical advice. Its named services are UK-based.
> **Used for:** Describing severe care needs, practical support at home, delayed PEM and activity tracking, and communicating changing care needs with a GP or social-care services.

## Highlights
- Describes how severe or very severe ME/CFS can require help with communication, sensory needs, food or drink, and 24-hour care.
- Says carers may need to maintain a support network, take breaks, and prioritize their own wellbeing. [[#^q2|passage]]
- Identifies communication with health and social-care services as part of supporting someone who is acutely affected.
- Lists listening, responsiveness, and tenderness as parts of caring.
- Says physical, mental, and emotional activities all use energy. [[#^q5|passage]]
- Notes that PEM-related symptom worsening may be delayed by 24 hours or more and describes recording daily activities. [[#^q6|passage]]
- Describes establishing an activity baseline, reducing activities that are too much, and waiting for symptoms to stabilize before increasing activity.
- Suggests keeping a diary and summarizing it for a GP to monitor changing needs or progress. [[#^q8|passage]]
- Says care planning with a GP may include home visits and regular monitoring, alongside social-care and carer support.

## Quoted passages

### Your own wellbeing as a carer

Caring is an admirable role, but it can be isolating. This is why it is so important to maintain or establish a good support network. Facing these challenges alone can impact your own health. Please ensure you take breaks for yourself, and prioritise your own wellbeing as much as is possible. ^q2

### Illness management

Physical, mental and emotional activities all use energy. Resting, sleeping, moving around, reading, texting, household chores, conversations, etc. are all activities that need to be taken into account when trying to manage ME/CFS. ^q5

Doing too much of an activity or engaging in an activity that is inappropriate, is likely to result in greater symptom severity and even relapse. Post-exertional malaise is such a key symptom of the condition that symptom exacerbations can be delayed by 24 hours or more. It is important to record each day’s activities and work out what seems possible based on your observations. ^q6

Keep a diary and try to establish some basic routines-you can summarise the diary when speaking with a GP and use it to monitor changing needs and/or progress. ^q8
