## Summary
The NIH intramural program ran a single-site, cross-sectional case-control study comparing 17 people with post-infectious ME/CFS (PI-ME/CFS) against 21 healthy volunteers across a very broad battery: autonomic testing, exercise and grip testing, brain imaging, cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), muscle biopsy, immune profiling, gene expression, metabolomics, and microbiome. The authors interpret their results as showing altered "effort preference" plus autonomic dysfunction, lower cardiorespiratory capacity, lower CSF catechols, and B-cell changes. The effort-preference framing drew a published Matters Arising critique (Davenport, Scheibenbogen, Zinn et al., Nat Commun 2025, PMID 41107289) and an author reply (PMID 41107314).

## Methods
Recruitment required medical-record documentation of persistent, severe fatigue and post-exertional malaise (PEM) that began after an acute infection within the previous five years, with no prior explanatory medical or psychiatric illness. A five-member panel of ME/CFS clinicians (Bateman, Kogelnik, Komaroff, Natelson, Peterson) had to agree unanimously on each case. Cases also had to meet at least one of three definitions (1994 Fukuda, 2003 Canadian Consensus Criteria, or IOM 2015) and have moderate-to-severe fatigue on the Multidimensional Fatigue Inventory. 27 candidates were evaluated in person and 17 were adjudicated as PI-ME/CFS; 21 healthy volunteers were recruited separately. Recruitment ended early because of the COVID-19 pandemic. Many sub-studies used subsets: for example, the single cardiopulmonary exercise test (CPET) included 8 patients and 9 volunteers. Protocol `NCT02669212`.

## Findings
- **Effort and motor performance:** On the Effort-Expenditure for Rewards Task, volunteers chose hard tasks more often than patients (OR 1.65). Maximal grip strength did not differ, but patients reached failure sooner on sustained grip, and this correlated with the effort-preference measure. Patients also showed less activation in the right temporal-parietal junction. The markers of peripheral and central fatigue the authors measured appeared in volunteers but not in patients.
- **Exercise capacity:** Peak VO2 was lower in patients (a difference of about 3.3 METs), with lower peak and higher resting heart rate. Chronotropic incompetence appeared in 5 of 8 patients versus 1 of 9 volunteers. The authors attribute the capacity gap to both chronotropic incompetence and deconditioning.
- **Autonomic:** 24-hour ECG showed lower heart rate variability (consistent with reduced parasympathetic tone) and lower baroreflex-cardiovagal function. Tilt testing of up to 40 minutes found no group difference in orthostatic hypotension or excessive tachycardia.
- **CSF:** Patients had lower levels of the catechols DOPA, DOPAC, and DHPG; norepinephrine and dopamine did not differ.
- **Immune:** Patients had a higher share of naïve B cells and a lower share of switched-memory B cells. The authors read this as consistent with chronic antigen stimulation.
- **Omics:** PBMC gene expression and metabolomic profiles differed from controls and differed by sex.
- **Negative findings:** No group differences in small-fiber nerve density, neuronal injury markers, polysomnography, body composition, or muscle-fiber atrophy ratio.
- **Misattribution:** The authors report that about 20% of cases that met study criteria later turned out to have other underlying causes.

## Limitations
- **Small sample:** 17 cases and 21 controls (n=38 total), and key sub-analyses used even smaller subsets (CPET 8 vs 9). The study is exploratory and many group comparisons in the figures are unadjusted.
- **Case definition:** Fukuda alone does not require PEM, but this cohort did require documented PEM at screening.
- **Single CPET:** The study used one CPET, not a 2-day CPET. The Matters Arising authors argue that this cannot capture the post-exertional decline that defines PEM, and that the controls were not matched for deconditioning. They dispute the conclusion that PEM reflects effort preference and deconditioning.
- **Generalizability:** The very strict adjudication (about 10% of reviewed candidates accepted) limits how far the results apply to the broader ME/CFS population.
- No treatments were tested.

## Abstract
Post-infectious myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (PI-ME/CFS) is a disabling disorder, yet the clinical phenotype is poorly defined, the pathophysiology is unknown, and no disease-modifying treatments are available. We used rigorous criteria to recruit PI-ME/CFS participants with matched controls to conduct deep phenotyping. Among the many physical and cognitive complaints, one defining feature of PI-ME/CFS was an alteration of effort preference, rather than physical or central fatigue, due to dysfunction of integrative brain regions potentially associated with central catechol pathway dysregulation, with consequences on autonomic functioning and physical conditioning. Immune profiling suggested chronic antigenic stimulation with increase in naïve and decrease in switched memory B-cells. Alterations in gene expression profiles of peripheral blood mononuclear cells and metabolic pathways were consistent with cellular phenotypic studies and demonstrated differences according to sex. Together these clinical abnormalities and biomarker differences provide unique insight into the underlying pathophysiology of PI-ME/CFS, which may guide future intervention. ^q1

### Limitations
This study has several limitations. This was a cross-sectional, exploratory case-control study. These data assess correlation, not causality. The sample size of the cohort was small but was very carefully characterized and was adequate for capturing group differences, noting important negative findings, and looking for patterns of consilience between measures. The small sample size may impact the precision of effect size measures, with a propensity to inflate estimates. Post-hoc calculations of the effect size for a phenotyping sample of 21 and 17 participants to achieve a power of 80% is 0.94, suggesting only large effects will be noted to be statistically significant. Overall, the groups are well-matched on demographic factors, but individual healthy volunteer and PI-ME/CFS participants were not always perfectly matched. Even though a major effort was made to minimize misattribution, we cannot completely exclude this possibility. ^q6
