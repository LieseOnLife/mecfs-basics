## Their account (paraphrased)

The carer describes their partner as having ME/CFS since 2024, without a formal diagnosis. From January 2024 through January 2026, the partner was described as moderate, housebound but mostly independent, needing cooking help and occasional assistance on stairs. In January 2026, the carer says the partner became severely ill within a week, bedbound and unable to get to the toilet without help. ^q1

For the three months before the April 23, 2026 post, the carer describes rolling PEM despite 24/7 rest; in the final week, the partner moved from severe to very severe. They could tolerate the carer's presence for no more than 5 minutes at a time and had difficulty eating: after a week unable to eat solid food, meal-replacement shakes were expensive and caused constipation, while porridge had been most of their food for about a month. ^q2

## What helped

The carer says noise-cancelling headphones initially helped with noise, but by the time of the post headphones and earplugs no longer blocked it completely. They were trying to return from shakes to porridge because the shakes were costly and constipating; the post does not describe a reliable way to stop the decline. ^q3

## What made it worse

The carer reports noise, light, smells, mental-health stress, and minor accidental injuries while doing tasks in complete darkness as factors after which the partner worsened. The home was on a noisy street and had dust and a cockroach problem; the household had no healthcare access, and financial pressure might require the carer to leave the partner alone for 8 hours a day. ^q4
