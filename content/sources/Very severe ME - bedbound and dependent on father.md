Cited in: [[Daily life and caring at home|Daily life and caring at home]]

## Their account (paraphrased)

The writer describes themself as extremely severely ill with ME/CFS, bedridden and completely dependent on their father for basic survival. They list sensory overload, chronic pain, malnutrition, movement symptoms, and POTS/dysautonomia among the health and care context. ^q1

They describe their father as sometimes kind and practically life-sustaining, but also exhausted and at times yelling, denying help, or refusing support the writer says they medically need. The writer says this mixture makes it difficult to separate caregiver burnout from harmful neglect, while still describing the effect as deeply harmful. ^q2

They say high stress and trying to suppress emotions to be easier to care for leaves them in constant PEM. They report needing help with positioning because of pressure sores, pain relief, medical advocacy, quiet support, and hydration. They have a J-tube but cannot tolerate enough fluid through it, while a PICC was denied because they already have a J-tube. ^q3

The writer says a public hospital does not feel safe or realistic because of trauma, sensory fragility, medical complexity, and physical vulnerability. Seeing a new person can cause crying from emotional overload; they say institutions they contacted did not respond or lacked capacity. ^q4

## What helped

The writer says their father has sometimes offered practical help and kept them alive; the post does not describe another available source of care. ^q5

## What made it worse

They report yelling, denied support, and stress as worsening their distress and PEM, while emotional suppression itself takes energy. They also describe difficulty accessing care that feels appropriate to their needs. ^q6
